# WWE Champions — a growth diagnosis

An independent product analysis of [WWE Champions](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/wwe-champions-wrestling-rpg/id1017432937) (Scopely), built from a firsthand new-player playthrough and a quantitative read of 2,945 public player reviews.

**[Read the presentation →](https://wwe-champions-growth.vercel.app)**  ·  **[Play the prototype →](https://wwe-champions-growth.vercel.app/prototype)**

---

## The argument

ARPDAU has two factors — `(payers ÷ DAU) × ARPPU`. On this title ARPPU is at its ceiling and defended; **payer rate has never been worked on**. The growth opportunity is therefore **first-purchase conversion for early-game spenders**, not extraction and not retention.

The evidence sits in two numbers. Across 2,945 reviews, the word *"fun"* appears at essentially the same rate in 1–2★ reviews (19.2%) as in 4–5★ reviews (20.3%) — a skew of **−1.1 percentage points** — while *"money"* is the most-used word in the whole corpus and skews **+26.7**. The loop is loved and the transaction is hated. People who don't care about spending don't talk about money.

And the transaction has almost no bottom rung. At League 2 the shelf leads with a $99.99 pack and its cheapest item is a **$14.99 starter pack on a 05h 42m countdown** — three to five times the conventional entry price for the genre, with nothing beneath it. The first thing the game ever asks you to buy is a rescue from a defeat.

> **Correction, 8 Aug 2026.** An earlier version said the shelf held *only* the $99.99 pack. Re-reading the source screenshot (`IMG_0821`) turned up the $14.99 starter pack beneath it. Move 02's modelled lift was cut from +0.55pp to +0.35pp as a result, and the programme's base case fell from $418k to $367k a year. Two prototype screenshot captions were also wrong and are fixed. The full log is in Act 09 of the evidence page.

The ordering principle for the whole portfolio:

> **Converting a non-payer buys you retention. Retaining a non-payer does not buy you a payer.**

An earlier version of this analysis recommended a retention-first portfolio. That portfolio grows revenue by growing DAU — the *denominator* — so it cannot raise ARPDAU. The rewrite is documented in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-06-first-purchase-conversion-design.md`.

## Method

**Firsthand play.** A new account played to League 2, capturing 42 screenshots of the activation window. Every claim in the analysis cites a specific screen, and captions were re-verified against the images before publishing.

**Review corpus.** 2,945 unique reviews — 474 from the iTunes customer-review feed, 2,471 from Google Play — sampled across newest, highest/lowest-rated, and most-helpful orderings, then de-duplicated. Analysis covers word frequency, keyword-family reach, and per-rating divergence.

**Population baseline.** Google Play exposes the full rating histogram: 402,333 ratings at 4.36★ (70% five-star, 8% one-star), against 50M+ installs. Only 8.1% of raters write text, which is why the written corpus skews negative — the histogram is the honest denominator, and the corpus is quoted only for *what people say*, never *how many feel it*.

**Evidence tiers.** Every claim is tagged as verified fact, player-reported signal, or a named data gap requiring internal telemetry. The tiering is deliberate: player sentiment is treated as a hypothesis to validate against funnel and cohort data, not as measurement.

## The model

Value estimates are a **transparent model, not a forecast**. The moves act on **ever-pay conversion** (the share of installs that ever make a purchase) and on **repeat rate**. Added payers are priced at the *marginal* converter's 180-day value, not the incumbent's, because adding a cheap rung raises conversion *and lowers* average first-purchase value — modelling them at the incumbent's value would be dishonest.

A power-law retention curve is still fitted through D1 and D7, but only to split total DAU into new-install and legacy contributions, and to price move 01's non-payer retention side-effect.

**One correction carried in from the previous model.** Its `base_dau` of 37,395 only reconciles with $800k/month at $0.22 ARPDAU if that figure is *DAU attributable to new installs* (90,000 × 12.5 user-days ÷ 30), not total DAU. Total DAU is ~121,000, including the 9.5-year legacy base. Ignorable when the metric was retention; load-bearing now, because ARPDAU is measured against the total.

Base case: ever-pay conversion 2.00% → 2.80%, **+$367k/yr and +2.2% ARPDAU**. The previously-recommended retention leads score **+$672k/yr and −6.0% ARPDAU** on the same baseline — a bigger revenue number that moves the metric the wrong way. That comparison is the recommendation.

Every input is editable in the page and lives in `data/roi-model.json`. Regenerate with `python3 scripts/build-model.py`.

## Limitations

- The review corpus is not a representative sample, by construction. It over-weights both tails.
- Onboarding complaints are only 3% of the corpus — which is expected, since players who churn on day one don't come back to write reviews. The cohort this analysis is about is **doubly silent**: day-one churners don't write, and neither do non-payers who looked at the shelf and closed it. Nobody writes a review about a purchase they didn't make.
- **The whole case rests on one store shelf, on one account, at League 2, on one day.** Storefronts are routinely geo- and cohort-tested. Confirming that observation is data pull 2, and the kill criterion is written into the proposal.
- Ever-pay conversion, first-purchase price distribution, repeat rate, retention curves, ARPDAU and drop rates are all internal and unavailable. Where a number is needed and absent, the analysis names the query rather than guessing.
